Middle Powers Leverage Map: Memo
Summary
We scored ten middle powers on seven layers of the AI supply chain and searched for small groups that together hold enough hard-to-replace levers to set a condition on frontier AI developers. Only two of 70 cells are chokepoints, both in chips: South Korea (memory) and the Netherlands (lithography). The best group of three is South Korea, Brazil and the Netherlands; the best group of four adds Singapore, a result that rests on one judgment-based cell (see Findings). We propose that such a group makes independent pre-deployment testing a condition of access to what it controls. The data is a first pass and the limits below apply.
The problem
Middle powers are asked to trust safety practices that frontier developers set alone, and they have no stated mechanism to ask for more. Statements of concern carry little weight without something to attach them to. The question here is narrower than "who is powerful": which countries hold something a developer cannot easily get elsewhere, and can any of those holdings be tied to a condition such as independent testing before deployment.
Method
Ten countries (Canada, South Korea, Brazil, Indonesia, Australia, Singapore, South Africa, the Netherlands, Nigeria, India) were scored on seven layers: Energy, Chips, Infrastructure (cables and exchanges), Data centres, Algorithms, Talent and Consumption. Each cell gets a score from 0 to 3 for how hard the lever is to replace, a confidence flag (Verified, Single source, Unverified), a Conditionable flag (Yes, Partly, No), a source link and date, and a one-line reason.
A score of 3 is reserved for a sole supplier or dominant share backed by a primary source or two agreeing sources. After the first pass, seven cells were re-scored under written rules so that similar evidence got similar scores; where sources conflicted, the lower score was used.
Groups of three or four are ranked in order by: chokepoint cells held, layers where the group reaches 2 or more, total coverage (the highest score any member holds in each layer, summed), and cells scoring 2 or more. The stability test re-runs the ranking with unverified and blank cells scored 1 instead of 0.

The group of three is not tied with another and stays on top when unverified and blank cells are scored 1. The group of four is decided by a tie-break: with Singapore and with India it ties on chokepoints, layers and coverage, and Singapore wins on cells scoring 2 or more (9 against 8). Singapore's edge rests on its Data centres score of 2, which depends on a documented overflow role into Indonesia. Its capacity is settled at 1,068 MW (Cushman & Wakefield, June 2026; the 1,650 MW in one page title is not in that page's text), but Singapore ranks only fifth in the region, so the score rests on the hub-role judgment. If that cell scored 1, the India group would lead. Every credible group contains South Korea and the Netherlands because they hold the only two chokepoints. Brazil adds Energy, Infrastructure and Consumption at 2. All ten Algorithms cells score 1, and nine of ten Data centres cells score 1, so no group here holds a hard-to-replace lever over models or compute capacity.

Safety impact
The proposal is that a group of middle powers attaches one condition, independent pre-deployment testing of frontier models, to the levers it controls. The map says where such a condition could bite: memory supply and lithography in chips, power and data-centre siting in Energy and Infrastructure, and market access in Consumption.
Three points make this a testable proposal and not a claim of power. First, the chokepoints sit with private firms, so governments can only partly condition them; the Conditionable flag records this cell by cell. Second, the levers outside chips score 2 at most, so a group built on them is a coalition of supporting levers, not of chokepoints. Third, a condition only matters if the countries agree to use it, which this data cannot show.
What the data does support is a short list of countries whose agreement would matter most, and a clear picture of which layers a testing condition could realistically attach to.
Limits
• Only two chokepoints exist in this data, both in chips, so the ranking mostly asks which extra members add other layers.
• All Algorithms cells score 1. No country here holds a hard-to-replace lever over frontier models.
• One cell has no score because no source was found (South Africa Talent). Nigeria Chips is scored 0 on a single self-reported claim, and Singapore Consumption is scored 1 from a second-hand report of Anthropic's own usage index.
• 58 of 70 cells are Single source or Unverified. Some figures were read through page summaries, not original documents.
• Scores are judgments under written rules; another reader could move a cell by one point.
• Ten countries is a sample, not a full map. Leverage held is not willingness to use it.
